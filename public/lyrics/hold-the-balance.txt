Oh, hold the balance lightly,
for the blade is cold and thin,
and the hand that holds the kingdom
may not hold the people in.

I press my name upon the page,
you wash it out with rain—
Oh, hold the balance lightly,
till the words return again.

Behind your glass the silver sleeps,
beneath the moon’s pale glow,
while we are turning winter fields
where only stones will grow.

And every stone that builds your wall
is lifted from the ground—
The taller stands the tower,
the deeper runs the sound.

Oh, hold the balance lightly,
for the blade is cold and thin.
The night may lean upon the dawn,
but dawn will still come in.

You drew a line across the hill
where no such line had been—
Oh, hold the balance lightly,
for the rain will wash it thin.

You gave the earth a borrowed name
and carved it into stone,
while I went westward with the tide
to find another home.

The stars were all the map I had,
the salt was on my skin—
Oh, hold the balance lightly,
I will return again.

Above the shore your banners flew,
black ribbons in the gale,
but underneath, a low song grew
no marching drum could quell.

You sealed one voice behind a door
and thought the night was still—
But one voice woke another voice
beyond the darkened hill.

And hill to hill there passed the word,
from harbour wall to fjord—
You may silence one among us,
but the song outlives the sword.

Oh, hold the balance lightly,
for the blade begins to sway.
The sea will cross your borders,
and the wind will fill our sails.

The tower trembles in its sleep
when the roots begin to sing.
Oh, hold the balance lightly—
for the wind serves neither king.

You closed your fist around the spark
to starve it through the night—
But every hand that shelters flame
will pass along the light.

So hold the balance lightly,
for the blade is cold and thin.
The tower trembles in its sleep
when the roots begin to sing.

The tower may command the sky,
the bell may name the hour,
but roots are moving through the earth
beneath the seat of power.

Oh, hold the balance lightly,
for the blade is cold and thin,
and the hand that holds the kingdom
may not hold the people in.
